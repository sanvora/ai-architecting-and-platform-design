# OsseoCore Technologies — CDAO Simulation

Welcome to the OsseoCore Technologies simulation. You play the role of the newly hired **VP, Data, Analytics, AI & Automation Officer (CDAO)** of OsseoCore Technologies — a fictitious standalone orthopaedics company separating from its parent conglomerate, Helix MedGroup.

Your job is to build a data, analytics, AI, and automation strategy from the ground up — designing the blueprints, navigating stakeholder dynamics, building the team, and producing the architectural deliverables that will define OsseoCore's data future.

**OsseoCore Technologies** is spinning off from **Helix MedGroup** to become the world's largest standalone orthopaedics-focused company. The separation will take 18–24 months. You start on Day 1 of the transaction close.

## The Three Persistent Tensions

These tensions run through every workstream and every stakeholder interaction:

### 1. Speed vs. Stability
- CEO and business sponsors want fast delivery and bold AI capabilities
- CIO and CFO want defensible timelines and sequenced investment
- Legal wants governance before deployment

**How it shows up:** Marcus Deveraux asks for AI ROI commitments; Priya Shenkar asks for cost models; Jonathan Okafor requires DPIAs. You must thread all three.

### 2. Centralized vs. Federated
- Layla Osei (CCO) wants to own her commercial analytics team and data
- Rodrigo Mendes (CSCO) wants supply chain data directly available without governance overhead
- Amara Diallo (Governance) wants central standards for all data products

**How it shows up:** Layla tries to hire her own analysts; Rodrigo bypasses the data product roadmap; Amara pushes governance-first sequencing.

### 3. Build vs. Buy
- Microsoft proposes Fabric as an alternative to your Databricks architecture
- Meridian Analytics offers pre-built analytics templates for commercial use cases
- Deloitte offers consulting accelerators for the carve-out

**How it shows up:** At every major architecture decision, a vendor offers to solve the problem for you. Evaluate on merits, not convenience.
